# Astrology-Tarot Synthesis

## The Golden Dawn Correspondences

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn established the most widely used mapping between tarot cards and astrological symbols. These correspondences form the bridge between the two systems.

## Court Card Elemental Assignments

Court cards combine two elemental energies:

| Rank | Element | Combined With Suit Element |
|------|---------|---------------------------|
| Page | Earth | Earth of Wands/Cups/Swords/Pentacles |
| Knight | Fire | Fire of Wands/Cups/Swords/Pentacles |
| Queen | Water | Water of Wands/Cups/Swords/Pentacles |
| King | Air | Air of Wands/Cups/Swords/Pentacles |

So the Knight of Cups is "Fire of Water" — passionate emotion, the romantic quest. The Queen of Swords is "Water of Air" — emotional intelligence applied to mental clarity.

## Practical Synthesis Techniques

### Technique 1: Tarot Card for a Transit

When a client is experiencing a challenging transit (e.g., Saturn conjunct natal Moon), pull a tarot card specifically for "how to navigate this transit." The card provides practical, actionable guidance that the abstract transit description may lack.

Example: Saturn conjunct natal Moon → The Hermit appears. Interpretation: "This transit calls for solitude and inner reflection. The Hermit shows that the emotional restriction you feel under Saturn is actually an invitation to deepen your inner life."

### Technique 2: Astrological Context for a Tarot Reading

When a tarot spread seems unclear, check the current astrological weather:
- What sign is the Moon in? This colors the emotional tone of the reading.
- Is Mercury retrograde? Cards about communication (3 of Swords, 8 of Wands) may carry extra weight.
- What house is the transiting Sun in? This is the area of life most illuminated right now.

### Technique 3: Birth Card Calculation

Reduce the Major Arcana to a birth card using the birth date (similar to numerology):
- Add all digits of the birth date until you get a number between 1 and 22.
- This card represents the person's life theme.
- Cross-reference with the card's astrological ruler (see table above) — it should resonate with the natal chart.

### Technique 4: Annual Card

Add the birth month and day to the current year:
- The resulting Major Arcana card is the person's "theme card" for the year.
- Compare it to the solar return chart. The themes should be parallel.

## When the Systems Agree

When astrology and tarot independently point to the same theme, the reading carries exceptional weight:
- A person going through a Pluto transit who draws Death (XIII) in a reading — both systems say transformation is non-negotiable.
- A person in a Venus return year who draws The Empress (III) — love, beauty, and fertility are the year's themes.
- A person with natal Saturn in the 10th house who draws The Emperor (IV) as their birth card — career authority and structural responsibility are life themes.

## When the Systems Diverge

Divergence is also informative:
- If the chart says "expand" (Jupiter transit) but tarot says "wait" (4 of Swords), there's a timing nuance. The expansion is coming, but preparation and rest come first.
- If the chart says "caution" (Saturn square) but tarot says "go" (Chariot), the querent's inner strength can overcome the external restriction.
- Never force agreement. Let each system speak in its own voice.
